Got questions about NAD+ supplements? Here are the answers to the most frequently asked questions.

## General Questions

### What is NAD+?
NAD+ (Nicotinamide Adenine Dinucleotide) is a coenzyme found in every living cell. It's essential for energy production, DNA repair, and hundreds of metabolic processes. NAD+ levels decline with age.

### What's the difference between NAD+, NMN, and NR?
- **NAD+:** The active molecule your cells use
- **NMN:** One conversion step away from NAD+
- **NR:** Two conversion steps away from NAD+

Think of it as: NR → NMN → NAD+

### Which is best: NMN or NR?
Both are effective. NMN is one step closer to NAD+, but NR has more human clinical trials. For most people, either works well. NMN is slightly preferred by the biohacking community.

### Do NAD+ supplements actually work?
Yes. Multiple human studies show that NMN and NR effectively raise blood NAD+ levels by 40-100%. Whether this translates to anti-aging benefits in humans is still being studied, but the biological mechanisms are well-established.

### How much NR should I take?

- Anti-aging: 300-600mg/day
- Tru Niagen recommends 300mg/day

### When should I take NAD+ supplements?
In the morning. NAD+ precursors can be energizing and may disrupt sleep if taken in the evening.

### Should I take them with food?
NMN: Can be taken with or without food. Empty stomach may improve absorption.
NR: Can be taken with or without food. No significant difference.

### Can I take NMN and NR together?
Yes, it's safe. However, there's no evidence it's better than taking one at an adequate dose. The body will convert whichever is available.

## Safety Questions

### Are NAD+ supplements safe?
Yes. Both NMN and NR have excellent safety profiles in human clinical trials. No serious adverse effects have been reported.

### What are the side effects?
Mild side effects may include nausea, headaches, or flushing. These are rare and usually resolve with dose adjustment. Add TMG to prevent headaches.

### Can I take NAD+ supplements with medications?
Generally yes, but consult your doctor if you take blood thinners, diabetes medications, or chemotherapy drugs.

### Are NAD+ supplements safe during pregnancy?
Insufficient data. Avoid during pregnancy and breastfeeding unless approved by your doctor.
